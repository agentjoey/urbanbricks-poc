# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Buyers in overseas English-speaking markets who need a building and have concluded that conventional construction is too slow, too expensive, or too unpredictable for what they want.

Two segments arrive with different projects and the same anxiety. Residential buyers want a home, cabin, or accessory dwelling. Commercial operators want a bar, an office, a camp, or another revenue-generating space. Which segment carries the business is an open question this site exists to answer — the lead form's project-type field is the instrument, not a formality.

Both arrive mid-doubt. They have usually seen a container building on social media, found the idea plausible, and now need to know whether it survives contact with reality: their budget, their site, their timeline, their local rules. They are not browsing for inspiration. They are checking whether this is real.

## Product Purpose

Collect qualified sales leads, and in doing so establish whether real demand exists for urbanbricks' modular buildings.

The site is a market experiment before it is a brochure. Success is not traffic; it is enough leads carrying enough intent signal — segment, timeline, budget band — to justify continuing or to justify stopping.

## Positioning

Your building takes 30 days — and it is being built while your site is.

Everything else about the company — the price, the finish, the warranty — is argued in service of that claim.

The precision matters more than the punchiness. Factory build time is 30 days (confirmed by Human Owner, 2026-07-21); groundwork, planning and connections are the customer's side of the schedule and are **not** included. Conventional construction runs those two phases in sequence, which is why it is measured in years. Modular runs them in parallel, so the building itself stops being the thing that takes the time.

**A headline that states a duration without stating what it covers is prohibited.** "Open in 30 days" is a claim urbanbricks cannot keep and a customer will hold it to; "built in 30 days, while your groundwork happens" is both true and a stronger argument, because it names the mechanism instead of asserting speed. Any copy that lets a visitor conclude they can move in 30 days after signing is a defect, not a shortcut.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary CTA: request a quote. Available inline on model pages, not only on the contact page — intent peaks while a visitor is looking at a specific building.
- Secondary CTA: browse the model library. For the visitor who is interested but not ready to hand over their details.
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: the building takes 30 days, it is made while the site is prepared, and someone is still answering the phone afterwards.
- Belief ladder, in order:
  1. This is a real category of building, not a novelty.
  2. It applies to my project specifically — my home, or my bar.
  3. The speed claim is credible because I can see the mechanism behind it.
  4. I can know the price before I commit to anything.
  5. Someone is accountable after handover.
  6. Asking for a quote costs me nothing and commits me to nothing.
- Proof on hand: none. There are no testimonials, case studies, completed-project photography, certifications, or delivery statistics available. Sections that would carry proof are built as empty structured slots. Fabricating any of it is prohibited — see Design Principles.

## Brand Personality

Crisp, optimistic, contemporary. The register of a furniture company that makes things well and does not need to shout about it, not of a construction firm performing gravitas.

Speed is the promise and calm is the delivery. Fast without panic: urgency belongs to the visitor's situation, never to the interface. Nothing counts down, nothing pressures, nothing is scarce for effect. Confidence reads as specificity — a date, a number, a named responsibility — rather than as adjectives.

The voice is plain. Short sentences, concrete nouns, no industry vocabulary that a first-time buyer would have to decode.

## Anti-references

Hard industrial. No steel-plate textures, no hazard yellow, no shipping-container stencil lettering, no cargo-orange-on-cool-grey. The container is how the building is made, not what the brand is about, and the industrial reflex is exactly what a visitor already expects from this category.

## Design Principles

**Show the mechanism, not the adjective.** "Fast" is a claim; parallel site prep and factory build is a reason. Every load-bearing promise is backed by the thing that makes it true, or it is not made.

**Certainty over enthusiasm.** Prefer a number, a date, or a named responsibility to a confident sentence. Where the number is not yet known, leave the structure and mark the gap rather than filling it with warmth.

**One narrative, both segments.** A visitor planning a bar and a visitor planning a home are buying the same thing: a building that arrives when promised. The site never forks into two microsites, and never makes either segment feel like the afterthought.

**Never borrow credibility.** The brand is new and has no history, no completed projects, and no reviews. Invented testimonials, fabricated case studies, decorative certification badges, and "500+ delivered" figures are prohibited outright — not deferred, not placeholdered. Trust is built from process transparency and specific warranty terms until real proof exists.

**Every page leads to a quote; no page traps the visitor there.** The primary CTA is always reachable and never the only way forward.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA as the build standard: contrast, semantics, keyboard operability, and visible focus are designed in from the first component rather than retrofitted. Interactive primitives come from shadcn/Radix so keyboard and ARIA behaviour is inherited rather than reimplemented.

Human Owner decision: AA is the construction standard but not a separate release gate for the POC — no dedicated audit run blocks a deploy at this stage. This is a deliberate deviation from the project workflow's non-waivable accessibility gate, taken because POC traffic is low; it is recorded so it can be revisited before the site carries real volume.

All motion respects `prefers-reduced-motion`. No state or meaning is communicated by colour alone.
